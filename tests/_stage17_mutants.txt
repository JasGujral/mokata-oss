⚠ A DECLARATION THIS LIST USED TO CARRY, NOW WITHDRAWN — recorded rather than deleted, because a
retracted claim teaches more than a claim that was quietly rewritten.

  The 19-mutant batch declared `human_approved=True` -> `assume_yes=True` an EQUIVALENT MUTANT that
  could not be caught: both flags clear the gate's human prompt, and the paths diverge only through
  the K3 trust dial, which `_migrate_vault` does not thread. Every word of that reasoning was
  about RUNTIME BEHAVIOUR, and as a statement about runtime behaviour it was correct — the review
  re-derived it independently and agreed the defect is not exploitable today.

  It was still the wrong conclusion, and the error is worth naming: the mutant was declared
  uncatchable because the only pins in scope asserted on OUTCOMES, and behaviour genuinely cannot
  separate the two flags here. What the batch did not ask was whether the CALL ITSELF could be
  pinned. It can — `TestTheBatchDecisionIsCarriedTRUTHFULLY` wraps a spy round the real
  `WriteGate.submit` and reads the flags the live call site submits, so a lie told to the gate is
  caught where it is told rather than only once a trust policy exists to punish it. M20-M22 all
  RED against those pins.

  THE LESSON: "no test can catch this" is a claim about the tests that exist, not about the code.
  A mutant that survives because the contract is asserted NOWHERE is a pin gap wearing an
  equivalence argument, and the equivalence argument is the more convincing of the two.

  What the original declaration got right and keeps: the defect is inert today, and the day the
  trust dial is threaded (SI.4-b, 0.0.18 stage 13) `human_approved=True` would clear propose-only
  with no human in the loop. That is why it was fixed before commit rather than filed.
